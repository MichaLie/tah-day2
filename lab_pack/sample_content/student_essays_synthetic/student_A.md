# Student A

The virtual learning environment (VLE) has been the most important digital technology in higher education over the last decade, and its effect has been overwhelmingly positive. Before VLEs, students had to collect handouts in person and email lecturers for every question. Now everything is in one place.

The main benefit is access. Students can watch recorded lectures at any time, which is especially valuable for those who work part-time or have caring responsibilities. Bayne and Ross (2019) argue that flexible access can widen participation for non-traditional students, and my own experience supports this: I would have struggled to attend every 9am lecture in person.

However, there are downsides. Some students never attend live sessions once recordings are available, and attendance at my university has clearly fallen because of lecture capture. This shows that the technology directly causes disengagement.

On balance, though, the VLE is positive because it puts the student in control of their own learning. A tool that gives students freedom is a good tool.

Word count: ~180. References: Bayne, S. and Ross, J. (2019). *The pedagogy of the digital university.*
