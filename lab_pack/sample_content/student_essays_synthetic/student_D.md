# Student D

Online proctoring software is the digital technology I have chosen. During the pandemic many universities adopted tools that watch students through their webcam during exams. I think this technology is negative overall.

The first problem is stress. Being watched by an algorithm that flags you for looking away is intimidating and makes it harder to concentrate. The second problem is fairness. These systems have been shown to work worse for some students than others, for example flagging students with darker skin or those in noisy home environments more often. This is a form of built-in bias.

There is also a privacy cost. Students are required to film their bedrooms and hand over biometric data to a private company, often with little say in the matter.

Supporters argue proctoring protects the value of the degree by preventing cheating. That is a real concern. But the same goal can be reached by redesigning assessment so that it is harder to cheat in the first place, without surveilling students.

Overall, the harms to fairness and privacy outweigh the benefit, so proctoring is a negative development. Universities should prefer assessment redesign to surveillance.
