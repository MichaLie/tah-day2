# Student B

This essay argues that generative AI chatbots have transformed higher education, and that the effect is mostly negative because they undermine independent thinking.

Generative AI became widely available to students very quickly. A recent survey found that 92% of students now use AI tools in their studies, and another study showed that 88% of essays are now written partly by AI. These numbers prove that the problem is out of control.

The core issue is cognitive offloading. When a student asks a chatbot for an answer, they skip the struggle that produces learning. Bastani et al. found that students with unrestricted AI access performed worse once the tool was taken away, which suggests the "learning" was an illusion. This is a serious concern for assessment validity.

Defenders say AI is just a tool like a calculator. But a calculator does one narrow thing, whereas a chatbot can produce the entire assignment, so the comparison does not hold.

In conclusion, generative AI has changed higher education for the worse, and universities should respond by returning to in-person examinations.

Word count: ~185. References: Bastani et al. (PNAS).
