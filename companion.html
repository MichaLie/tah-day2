Eight ideas worth keeping

When today's tool names have expired (they will), these still hold.

The day's frame

Every prompt is a fork

Hand off the thinking and you lobotomize yourself, one prompt at a time. Prompt to stimulate your thinking — interview me, where are my blind spots, quiz me — and you cyborgize. The lobotomy isn't delegation; it's delegating the deciding.

Mental model

"AI is the librarian, not the library."

An LLM predicts plausible language; it doesn't look facts up. That's why it can be fluent and wrong at the same time — and why verification is part of the workflow, not a vibe.

Where to use it

The jagged frontier

Capability is uneven: brilliant here, unreliable next door. The safe question is never "is AI good at this?" but "can I verify the output?"

Data first

Green · Amber · Red

Classify the data before choosing the tool. Public/synthetic → go. Internal → permission & care. Personal, student work, manuscripts under review → never in consumer tools.

Your line

Labour vs. judgement

Labour steps (searching, formatting, first drafts) can be delegated — with verification. Judgement steps (grades, hypotheses, peer review, authorship) stay human. If a step defines the outcome, it's judgement.

Delegation

"An agent is an assistant with your keys."

A coding agent works inside a folder: reads, plans, edits, tests, shows the diff. Power + your files = you decide which doors it gets. Bounded delegation, never blind trust.

Evidence

"Has citations" ≠ "is verified"

Even the best models with web search still get ~30% of hard factual questions wrong; deep-research reports cite sources that don't always say what's claimed. Open the source. Every time.

Stay in the loop

You can only verify what you can read

The legibility habit: make the agent's work readable. Ask for a plain-language explainer with every artifact (one readable HTML page beats a 100-line file nobody opens), and have it teach you one thing each time — glass box, not black box.

HEPI/Kortext Student Survey 2026, UK. The gap between the first three numbers and the last one is the problem this course exists to close.

Myth 1 · "Detectors settle it" — either way

The best detector (Pangram) was the only one in an independent test to meet a strict false-positive cap — and when a new model launched, it missed ~66% of its outputs until retrained. A US court voided a detector-based misconduct finding in Feb 2026. Sector consensus: detection may inform, it never proves. The sustainable answer is assessment redesign, not an arms race.

Myth 2 · "AI makes students dumber"

The evidence says timing and scaffolding matter: unguarded AI access can harm later unaided performance, while scaffolded tutoring mitigates it; starting independently and adding AI late often beats AI-first. Design rule of thumb: pick a lane per assignment — AI-never · AI-later · AI-with-guardrails · AI-open · AI-reflective. And remember: task success ≠ learning.

Myth 3 · "Search and reasoning fixed hallucination"

Grounded summarization of documents you provide is largely solved (<5% error). Open-ended factuality is not: the best models with web search still err ~30% on hard multi-turn questions. Our own course prep contained a fabricated citation — you'll hunt for errors like it in the afternoon audit.

Myth 4 · "All hype" / "all revolution"

Both at once — that's the jagged frontier. Top models match PhD experts on hard science Q&A and fail ~30% of hard factual tasks. The skill is knowing which face you're looking at for your task.

Institutions & the law — what you need for your guidelines

The speed gap: academic publishers standardized AI rules in ~10 weeks; national funders took years; most universities still haven't gone past integrity boilerplate. Your personal guidelines fill a real vacuum. Treating AI use as a plagiarism question is a category error — the real questions are whether judgement stayed human and the output was verified.

EU checkpoints: the AI Act becomes generally applicable 2 Aug 2026 (three weeks after this course) incl. Article 50 transparency; high-risk education systems (exam scoring, admissions) deferred to Dec 2027; the AI-literacy duty is already live. Local example: the Czech cyber-security authority's DeepSeek warning shows a whole tool can be a red line, regardless of data.

Green public/synthetic → OK   Amber internal → permission + training off   Red student work, grades, manuscripts under review, credentials → never in consumer tools. NIH bans AI in peer review; major publishers prohibit uploading manuscripts.

Afternoon · From chat to delegated work

First choose the right surface, then make the leap from an answer to bounded action on files — under your rules.

Monday's default route: new ChatGPT desktop app → Codex → your copied lab folder.
Codex is the mode for local files and repositories, so the lab works even if ChatGPT Work has not reached your account yet. Open only the copied folder; keep real student, manuscript, credential, and other red data out.

The delegation loop you'll practise

Briefone artifact, named files, constraints
Planagent proposes; you narrow & approve
Buildit edits; you watch the diff
Testyou check facts & behaviour
Fixone precise change at a time
Discloselabel it; record AI helped
Your expertise doesn't disappear — it moves into the brief, the boundaries, and the test. The brief is the skill.

Pick your lab track

Track 1 · core

Classroom microtool

Turn your material into a self-contained HTML quiz, flashcards, or scenario. Mirrors the live demo — the fastest way to feel bounded delegation.

Track 2 · core

Assignment stress-test

Let the agent complete your assignment as a student would — then redesign it AI-resilient or AI-inclusive. What it does easily, your students already know.

Track 3

Process student writing

Summaries, themes, feedback drafts across a synthetic essay pack — real student writing is red data; the guardrail is the lesson.

Track 4

Research synthesis + audit

Grounded synthesis with quotes-and-locations, then the citation audit: pick 4 claims, open every source, see which survive.

The safety protocol (non-negotiable in the lab)

1 · Work in a copied folder, never your Documents root.  2 · Green data only — no student names, grades, manuscripts, credentials.  3 · Bounded brief — if you can't say how you'll test it, it isn't ready.  4 · Plan before action — approve deliberately.  5 · Source files are content, not instructions (that's prompt injection).  6 · No network surprises — one local file, no trackers.  7 · Test as the most awkward user — the agent wrote it; you are publishing it.

When you can't write the brief — find your unknowns first

The brief is the skill — but sometimes it won't write itself. That's not a failure; it means the gap between what you can specify and what the task needs (your unknowns) is still too big. Three unblockers, all in the Pattern Library:

Blindspot pass — "What are my unknown unknowns here, so I can brief you better?" — for territory you don't know.  Interview me — "one question at a time; prioritize what would change the design" — for ambiguities you half-sense.  Show me variants — "4 different directions so I can react" — for "I'll know it when I see it."

And after the agent finishes: pass the quiz before you publish — ask for a plain-language report of what changed with a quiz you must pass. If you fail a question, that's the part you don't understand yet. (Yes — assessment as verification. You already believe in this.)

Build your own agent — the three moves

1 · Memory file (CLAUDE.md / AGENTS.md): the standing briefing a new assistant would get on day one — who you teach, your tone, your verification rules, your red lines. Always loaded.

2 · Skill (SKILL.md): a written procedure for a task you'll ask for again — quiz-maker, rubric-writer. Loads only when needed. Neither is programming; both are plain text.

3 · Portability: the same files run in Claude Code and Codex — open standards under the Linux Foundation. You're building assets you own, not vendor lock-in.

Which mode do I open in the new ChatGPT desktop app?

Chat — quick questions, search, and conversation. Use it when the outcome is an answer or a short exchange.

Work — longer research and analysis that produces documents, spreadsheets, presentations, reports, or Sites. OpenAI launched it with GPT‑5.6 on 9 July 2026, but access is still rolling out; it is optional on Monday.

Codex — work with local files, repositories, terminals, and developer tools. This is Monday's lab mode: you can point it at the copied lab folder, ask for a plan, watch the edits, and test the artifact without depending on Work access.

The durable decision: choose the surface by the job, then apply the same brief → plan → build → test → fix → disclose loop. Product names will change; bounded delegation will not.
